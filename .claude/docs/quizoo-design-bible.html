01

What we are building

Read this section, then the token sections, then the screens.

Quizoo is a single-player-first trivia game. The distinction matters: a quiz app tests you, a game rewards you. Every decision below serves one loop — pick a topic → survive a timed level → get paid in points, coins and status → see how far the next level is. Sign-in is never a gate; it is the offer we make the moment a player has something worth saving.

Five product pillars

Pillar 01

Zero-friction first play

No splash gate, no register wall. A guest can tap a tile and be answering a question within 8 seconds of first launch. Progress is written locally from question one.

Pillar 02

Pressure you can feel

The timer ring is the app's heartbeat. It shrinks with level: 30s → 25s → 20s → 15s. Under 5 seconds the whole screen shifts to the danger palette and the haptic ticks.

Pillar 03

Always one step visible

The level map always shows the next locked node and exactly what unlocks it. Never end a session on a dead end — every result screen offers a next action.

Pillar 04

Status is the real currency

Coins buy power-ups; badges, ranks and league position buy bragging rights. Leaderboards are per-category so everyone can be top of something.

Pillar 05

Native in five languages

English, Urdu, Arabic, Hindi and Spanish, with full RTL mirroring and Nastaliq typography for Urdu — not a bolted-on translation layer.

Anti-pillar

What we will not do

No energy timer that blocks play. No interstitial ad between every question. No leaderboard that only the top 100 can see themselves on. No dark-pattern purchase prompts on a loss screen.

Rules

  • Question text gets text-wrap: balance and a hard 3-line cap; longer questions drop to 17px before they ever truncate. A truncated question is a bug, not a layout.
  • Numbers that stack — scores, ranks, timers, leaderboard points — always use font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums.
  • Never below 10.5px. Meta labels get +4% letter-spacing to survive at that size.
  • Urdu and Arabic need ~2.1 line-height and 4px more vertical padding in every button and option row. Build this into the theme, not into individual screens.
  • Ship the four families as bundled assets (google_fonts at runtime is a cold-start network dependency you do not want on the splash screen).

The 4px hard shadow is the signature. Every pressable surface — buttons, answer options, level nodes — sits on a solid, un-blurred 4px ledge in a darker shade of its own colour, and translates down 4px on press. That single detail is what makes the app read as a toy rather than a form.

Edge-to-edge & safe areas

Every screen draws behind the status bar. A screen's own background — the splash gradient, the category header, the dark play arena, a result screen's colour wash — must run to the physical top of the display, never stop at the status-bar line. In Flutter: SystemChrome.setSystemUIOverlayStyle(SystemUiOverlayStyle(statusBarColor: Colors.transparent, …)), SystemChrome.setEnabledSystemUIMode(SystemUiMode.edgeToEdge), and no SafeArea wrapping the background layer — only the content layer takes MediaQuery.padding.top as its top inset. The status-bar icon brightness is a per-route property: set statusBarIconBrightness from the colour underneath it, which is why the coloured screens in this document render white status text and the neutral ones render dark.
A screen's sections must never shrink to fit. The scrolling body is a column, and a column's children shrink by default — so once a screen's content is taller than the viewport, every section gets squashed and the cards inside are clipped mid-content. Pin them: flex-shrink: 0 on each child of the scroll body in these frames, and in Flutter a ListView/CustomScrollView whose children size themselves, never a Column inside an Expanded that has to distribute a fixed height. Content taller than the screen is supposed to overflow and scroll; it is not supposed to compress.

Same rule at the bottom: the tab bar and any bottom CTA sit above MediaQuery.padding.bottom, and the bar is always pinned to the bottom of the viewport — never pushed up by short content. In the frames below it is margin-top: auto; in Flutter it is the bottomNavigationBar slot of Scaffold, not the last child of a Column.

S-06Search

Searches subcategories and question tags. Debounced at 250 ms, results are local-first from the cached taxonomy so it works offline. Breadcrumb shows the parent category.

Home is a stack of category sections (rev. 12 Sep). The category chip row is gone. Filtering to one category hid the other thirteen, and a first-launch guest could not see how much was in the app. Instead every category renders as its own section — medallion, name, topic count — with its subcategories on a horizontal rail of .sub2 cards beneath it and a hairline between sections. All 14 render, in the order the taxonomy endpoint returns, with the player's most-played category promoted to the top.
  • The section header is always tappable and opens that category's screen (S-07). It is the guaranteed route in, which is why it is a row and not a label.
  • More than ten subcategories additionally shows a .seeall pill in the header and a dashed .sub2.more end-cap at the end of the rail. Ten or fewer shows a plain N topics › count — the rail already holds them all. The backend computes this flag; the client never counts.
  • Section order is state-driven, as S-05 already was: daily → continue → popular → categories.
  • One subcategory card everywhere. The same .sub2 renders at 164px on a home rail and as a two-up cell on the category screen — never a separate smaller tile. Its state line is 400 questions · New when untouched and Lv 12 · ⭐⭐ over a filled bar once started; the card never changes height between the two.
  • The chip row survives inside Search (S-06) only, where filtering is the actual job.
On the ten-topic threshold. No category in the taxonomy at §21 has more than eight subcategories, so on today's content the See all pill never appears — S-05 draws Sports at twelve purely to specify that state. Until a category grows past ten the header row is the only route to S-07, which is why it must be tappable rather than decorative. If you would rather the pill always showed, that is a one-line change to the flag, not to the layout.

Every level is 20 questions. Levels 5, 10, 15 and 20 are boss levels: same rules, double payout, crown on the node. Hearts are the only fail condition. Per-question score

// evaluated identically on client (for display) and server (for record)
base        = 100
speedBonus  = round(100 × secondsLeft / secondsTotal)      // 0 … 100
diffMult    = 1 + (level − 1) × 0.05                       // L1 = 1.00 … L20 = 1.95
streakMult  = streak >= 12 ? 2.0
            : streak >=  8 ? 1.8
            : streak >=  5 ? 1.5
            : streak >=  3 ? 1.2 : 1.0

questionScore = round((base + speedBonus) × diffMult × streakMult)

// worked example — Level 12, answered with 14 of 20 seconds left, 5-answer streak
(100 + 70) × 1.55 × 1.5 = 395 points

Leaderboard periods & leagues

  • Weekly boards reset Sunday 00:00 UTC. Monthly reset on the 1st. All-time never resets. Every board exists globally, per country and per subcategory.
  • Leagues are separate from boards: 30 players of similar weekly XP are pooled. Top 10 promote, bottom 5 relegate, the middle stays. Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum → Diamond → Legend.
  • Guests appear on no board. Their local score is preserved and merged on first sign-in.
  • Anti-cheat floor: any level with a mean answer time under 1.0 s, or a score above the theoretical maximum for that level, is recorded but excluded from boards and flagged. The server recomputes every submitted level from the answer log — the client's own total is display only.

Question authoring contract

  • Exactly 4 options for text questions, 3 for image questions (the plate eats the vertical room).
  • Every question carries a one-sentence explanation — it is shown on both correct and wrong feedback and is the app's only teaching surface. No explanation, no publish.
  • difficulty is 1–5, seeded by the author then recalibrated nightly from live pass rates. A question whose live pass rate drifts more than 25 points from its band is auto-requeued for review.
  • Distractors must be plausible. A question where three options are absurd is a free point, and free points break the difficulty curve.
  • Translations are separate rows keyed to the same question_id, each independently reviewable. A missing translation silently drops the question from that language's pool — never shows English to an Urdu player.

The recommended stack, concretely

Data plane — Supabase

Postgres · Auth · Realtime · Storage · Edge Functions

  • Postgres holds taxonomy, questions, translations, progress, scores, duels, economy ledger
  • Auth with Google, Apple, Facebook + anonymous guest, linked on sign-in
  • Row Level Security so a client can never write another player's score
  • Storage + CDN for question images, audio clips, category art, avatars
  • Edge Functions (Deno) for anything needing a secret: IAP receipt validation, ad-reward callbacks, matchmaking
  • pg_cron for weekly board rollovers, league assignment, difficulty recalibration, daily challenge generation
Client services — Firebase

FCM · Crashlytics · Analytics · Remote Config

  • FCM for streak reminders, duel challenges, league results — free at any scale you will reach
  • Crashlytics — non-negotiable for a Flutter app with heavy animation
  • Analytics for funnel: install → first level → level 3 → sign-in → day-7 return
  • Remote Config to tune the economy without shipping: coin payouts, timer seconds, sign-in prompt timing
  • A/B Testing on exactly one thing at first: when the save-progress sheet appears

Why not a custom Node or Flask backend for v1

Nothing here needs it. You would be rebuilding auth, social login, token refresh, RLS, realtime transport, file storage, CDN and a migration story — several weeks of work that produces no player-visible value, plus permanent on-call. Supabase is Postgres: if you outgrow it, you take a pg_dump and run the same schema anywhere. That is a much cheaper exit than migrating off Firestore's document model.

When to revisit. Build custom services when one of these is true: (1) duel concurrency passes ~5,000 simultaneous matches and you want a dedicated authoritative game server; (2) you need question delivery logic complex enough to be its own service, e.g. per-player adaptive difficulty with an ML model; (3) a compliance requirement forces data residency Supabase's regions don't cover. Until then, an Edge Function is the right size of new backend.

Row Level Security — the rules that matter

  • profiles: a user may update only their own row, and only the columns username, avatar_key, country_code, locale. Coins, XP, hearts and elo are revoked from the client entirely and moved only by security-definer functions.
  • level_attempts: insert own rows only; server_score, stars and flagged are computed by a before insert trigger, not accepted from the client.
  • score_events: no client write at all. Written by the same trigger.
  • questions / question_i18n: select only, and only where status = 'live'. Never ship correct_index to the client before the answer is submitted — serve levels through an RPC that returns options with the correct index shuffled per attempt and stored server-side against the seed.
  • duels: readable only by p1 and p2; answer arrays writable only for your own side, and only while status = 'active'.

Duel architecture

Fair on a bad network, cheap on the server.
1. QUEUE      Client calls matchmake → row in matchmaking_queue (user, elo, band).
              Function pairs the two closest ELOs inside the band, widening ±40 every 6 s.
              After 20 s: create a duel with is_bot = true and a labelled bot opponent.

2. DEAL       Server creates the duel row with question_ids[5] and pushes it to both
              clients over a Realtime channel duel:<id>. Both receive the identical
              five questions and their options up front, answer keys withheld.

3. PLAY       Each client runs its own 10-second clock per round, locally.
              On answer: broadcast {round, answeredAt_ms} — the choice itself is NOT
              broadcast, only that you answered and how fast. This is what lets the
              opponent's "thinking…" indicator be live without leaking anything.
              The choice goes to the server: duels.p{n}_answers append.

4. REVEAL     When both answers for a round land (or both clocks expire), the server
              writes the round result and broadcasts it. Correctness is revealed to
              both players simultaneously. Round point goes to the correct answer;
              if both are correct, to the faster time; if both wrong, nobody scores.

5. RESOLVE    resolve-duel runs on round 5, on expiry, or on a 30-second presence gap.
              Elo:  R' = R + K × (S − E),  K = 32,  E = 1 / (1 + 10^((Ro − R)/400))
              Payout: winner +200 🪙 +250 XP · loser +40 🪙 +80 XP.

DISCONNECT   Presence drop ≥ 30 s forfeits only the remaining rounds, not the match.
              A player who was 3–0 up and loses signal still wins 3–2. Reconnect inside
              30 s resumes at the current round with the clock reset for that round only.
              Repeated disconnects (>3 in 24 h) suppress ELO gain — rage-quit insurance.
Cost note. A duel is one row, two channel subscriptions and about a dozen small broadcasts. At 5,000 concurrent duels that is well inside a single Supabase Realtime instance. If you ever exceed it, the fix is a dedicated Elixir or Go matchmaker in front of the same Postgres — not a rewrite.

Architecture

  • Bloc/Cubit throughout, as in your existing app. One QuizBloc owns the entire level: question index, timer ticks, streak, mistakes, power-ups. It emits a single immutable QuizState — the UI never computes score.
